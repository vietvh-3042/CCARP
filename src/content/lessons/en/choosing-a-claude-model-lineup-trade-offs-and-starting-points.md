tabular-nums font-normal text-muted-foreground">4<!-- -->.</span>Tiering models inside one system</h2><p class="mt-4 leading-relaxed text-foreground">Using one model for every request means paying frontier prices on easy work or getting small-model quality on hard work. Multi-model designs avoid both by sending the expensive model only the decisions that need it. Most tokens are then billed at the lower rate, and frontier capability goes to the hard cases where cheaper models fail.</p><p class="mt-4 leading-relaxed text-foreground">There are two common patterns, and they suit different problems:</p><ul class="mt-4 leading-relaxed text-foreground list-disc space-y-1 pl-6"><li class="pl-1"><strong class="font-semibold">Executor with an advisor.</strong> A lower-cost model does the work and consults a frontier model to check its plan or review its output. This helps when the cheaper model stalls only on hard decisions. The advisor only helps when it is consulted, so first price the cheaper model at low effort on its own and measure how often it consults. In one measurement, Sonnet 5 with a Fable 5 advisor came within 10% of Fable 5's SWE-bench Pro score at 63% of the price of running Fable 5 alone. Advisor choice is restricted: the advisor tool rejects some models as advisors, including Claude Haiku 5.5.</li><li class="pl-1"><strong class="font-semibold">Orchestrator with workers.</strong> A capable model plans the work and hands the bulk of it to lower-cost workers. This fits work that is too large for one context window. Sonnet's balance of speed and cost makes it a common worker, and Haiku is aimed at sub-agent tasks.</li></ul><p class="mt-4 leading-relaxed text-foreground">A third pattern is <a class="text-primary underline underline-offset-2 hover:no-underline" href="/en/lessons/task-decomposition-with-workflow-patterns-chaining-routing-parallelization/">Routing</a>. A classifier sorts incoming requests before any work starts, sends easy ones to a small model and hard ones to a large one. Routing is a cost pattern only if the classifier is reliable. A hard request sent to a small model becomes a failed task, and as the previous section showed, failed tasks drive the bill. Like every model choice, a tiered design proves its value through cost per solved task on your own eval set. Fewer frontier tokens alone do not show that it is cheaper.</p><aside class="my-6 rounded-lg border border-dashed border-border bg-muted/20 p-4"><p class="flex items-center gap-1.5 text-xs font-semibold uppercase tracking-wide text-muted-foreground"><svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" width="24" height="24" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" class="lucide lucide-circle-help h-3.5 w-3.5 shrink-0" aria-hidden="true"><circle cx="12" cy="12" r="10"></circle><path d="M9.09 9a3 3 0 0 1 5.83 1c0 2-3 3-3 3"></path><path d="M12 17h.01"></path></svg>Apply it to your own work</p><p class="mt-1.5 text-sm leading-relaxed text-foreground">Think of a workload you know.
