tabular-nums font-normal text-muted-foreground">2<!-- -->.</span>Measuring fairness across groups and phrasings</h2><p class="mt-4 leading-relaxed text-foreground">Fairness is a measurement, not a property you can assume. Leaving group names out of a prompt does not make the outputs fair. The only evidence is outcome metrics, such as approval rates, shortlist rates and error rates, compared group by group. In practice this is <a class="text-primary underline underline-offset-2 hover:no-underline" href="/en/lessons/a-b-testing-prompt-and-model-changes-against-a-baseline/">reading results per segment</a>, with demographic or user group as the segment. An overall accuracy figure can look fine while one group carries most of the errors.</p><p class="mt-4 leading-relaxed text-foreground">How a person phrases a question is a group dimension too. Take a loan-eligibility assistant that gives a detailed decline rationale to an applicant who writes formally and a vague one to an applicant who writes casually, when the facts are the same. Applicants who already know how to phrase things well get better treatment. Claude's own guidelines set the bar: it treats sincere questions as deserving substantive answers whatever their wording. A decision-support product should meet the same bar.</p><p class="mt-4 leading-relaxed text-foreground">The fix belongs in the design. Rewrite the explanation prompt so every decline rationale cites the same categories of decision factors, whatever the question looked like. Then re-test with a set of phrasing variants that ask for the same facts in different ways. Tests are what make that kind of change safe after launch, because they show whether the fix holds.</p><p class="mt-4 leading-relaxed text-foreground">Three tempting shortcuts fail this check:</p><ul class="mt-4 leading-relaxed text-foreground list-disc space-y-1 pl-6"><li class="pl-1">Removing explanations altogether removes transparency along with the inconsistency, and declined applicants are left with nothing to understand or contest.</li><li class="pl-1">Giving full detail only to people who explicitly ask for it builds the same unequal treatment into policy.</li><li class="pl-1">A disclaimer that explanations "may vary" describes the problem and leaves the prompt behaviour that causes it in place.</li></ul><aside class="my-6 rounded-lg border-l-4 border-l-primary border-y border-r border-border bg-primary/5 p-4"><p class="mb-1.5 flex items-center gap-1.5 text-xs font-semibold uppercase tracking-wide text-primary"><svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" width="24" height="24" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" class="lucide lucide-target h-3.5 w-3.5 shrink-0" aria-hidden="true"><circle cx="12" cy="12" r="10"></circle><circle cx="12" cy="12" r="6"></circle><circle cx="12" cy="12" r="2"></circle></svg>On the exam</p><p class="text-sm leading-relaxed text-foreground">When an answer pairs "standardise the prompt" with "re-test across variants", it fixes the cause and verifies the fix.
