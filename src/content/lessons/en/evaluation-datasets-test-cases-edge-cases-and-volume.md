text-foreground" data-lesson-section=""><span class="mr-2 tabular-nums font-normal text-muted-foreground">1<!-- -->.</span>Success criteria come before test cases</h2><p class="mt-4 leading-relaxed text-foreground">An evaluation dataset is only useful if it measures something definite. Before anyone writes a test case, the team needs success criteria that say what good output looks like, because every case and every grader exists to check one of them. Choosing which dimensions matter, such as accuracy, latency, cost or safety, belongs to <a class="text-primary underline underline-offset-2 hover:no-underline" href="/en/lessons/latency-cost-safety-and-security-metrics/">defining evaluation metrics</a>. This lesson starts one step later: the shape a criterion needs before a dataset can test it.</p><p class="mt-4 leading-relaxed text-foreground">A criterion is testable when it has four properties:</p><ul class="mt-4 leading-relaxed text-foreground list-disc space-y-1 pl-6"><li class="pl-1"><strong class="font-semibold">Specific</strong>: it names the behaviour. "Accurate sentiment classification" is specific; "good performance" is not.</li><li class="pl-1"><strong class="font-semibold">Measurable</strong>: it uses a number, or a qualitative scale applied consistently alongside numbers. Even safety can be counted, for example fewer than 0.1% of outputs out of 10,000 trials flagged for toxicity.</li><li class="pl-1"><strong class="font-semibold">Achievable</strong>: the target comes from industry benchmarks, prior experiments or expert knowledge, and stays within what current frontier models can do.</li><li class="pl-1"><strong class="font-semibold">Relevant</strong>: it fits the application's purpose. Citation accuracy can be critical for a medical app and matter much less for a casual chatbot.</li></ul><p class="mt-4 leading-relaxed text-foreground">A rewrite shows the difference. "The model should classify sentiments well" fails all four. The good version asks for an F1 score of at least 0.85 on a held-out test set of 10,000 diverse Twitter posts, a 5% improvement over the current baseline. That criterion already describes a dataset: how big it is, where it comes from, and that it is held out. A criterion written this way tells you what to build next.</p><p class="mt-4 leading-relaxed text-foreground">Run the same check on a draft like "summaries should be concise and helpful." Rewritten as "average at least 4 out of 5 on a completeness rubric across a 500-claim sample," it becomes a number you can check on every run, and it holds up: a rubric scale is a legitimate qualitative measure, and an average leaves room for imperfect individual outputs. Three other rewrites look like progress but still fail. "Sound less robotic" is a preference with no measurement attached. "A perfect 5 from every reviewer" is measurable but not achievable, because it demands flawless agreement that no baseline supports. "Every summary signed off by a senior reviewer" is a review process, not a criterion, and it puts a human into the grading of every run.</p><aside class="my-6 rounded-lg border border-dashed border-border bg-muted/20 p-4"><p class="flex items-center gap-1.5 text-xs font-semibold uppercase tracking-wide text-muted-foreground"><svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" width="24" height="24" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" class="lucide lucide-circle-help h-3.5 w-3.5 shrink-0" aria-hidden="true"><circle cx="12" cy="12" r="10"></circle><path d="M9.09 9a3 3 0 0 1 5.83 1c0 2-3 3-3 3"></path><path d="M12 17h.01"></path></svg>Apply it to your own work</p><p class="mt-1.5 text-sm leading-relaxed text-foreground">Pick a feature you are building. text-foreground">Your criteria tell you what to test, and production tells you what mix of inputs to test it on. A dataset of only easy, well-formed inputs will score well and predict little. Automated evals can create false confidence when they don't match real usage patterns. So the first job is to reproduce the inputs the system actually receives: the same document types, lengths, phrasing and noise, in roughly the same proportions.</p><p class="mt-4 leading-relaxed text-foreground">Next, add edge cases on purpose. They are rare in any sample, and failures tend to hide there. A summarization set adds multitopic articles and misleading headlines. An FAQ consistency set adds typos, long rambling questions and irrelevant personal details. A medical-privacy set adds health information that is explicit, hypothetical ("if my friend Alice, born on…") and implicit ("the same medication as his father"). Every case also needs a golden answer. That is either a mandatory exact match, or an example of a perfect answer that gives the grader something to compare against.</p><p class="mt-4 leading-relaxed text-foreground">Take a clause-extraction feature tested only on clean, standard commercial leases. applications need to be evaluated on several success criteria at once, and that is why mixed methods exist. The sentiment model from the earlier criterion is judged on F1, the share of non-toxic outputs, how severe its errors are, and response time, all on the same held-out set. No single grader checks all four, so one task usually carries several graders, each checking one part of the output.</p><p class="mt-4 leading-relaxed text-foreground">There are three ways to combine those graders into one verdict. <strong class="font-semibold">Weighted</strong> scoring requires the combined scores to reach a threshold. <strong class="font-semibold">Binary</strong> scoring requires every grader to pass. A <strong class="font-semibold">hybrid</strong> mixes the two, for example making a privacy check a hard gate while weighting tone and completeness together. Graders can also look at different evidence. A flight-booking agent may say "Your flight has been booked" in its transcript, but the outcome is whether a reservation actually exists in the database. A code check on the outcome catches what a rubric reading the transcript would accept.</p><p class="mt-4 leading-relaxed text-foreground">In a mixed framework, humans do a specific job: calibration. Descript's video-editing agent followed this path. It moved from manual grading to LLM graders using criteria defined by the product team, with periodic human calibration.
